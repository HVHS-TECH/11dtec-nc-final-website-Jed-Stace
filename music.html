The Origins of Music:

Music is one of humanity's oldest forms of expression, with evidence of instruments dating back over 40,000 years. The earliest known bone flutes and drums were used in ritual, storytelling, and community gathering. As civilisations developed, so did their musical traditions from the hymns of ancient Mesopotamia to the elaborate court music of China and the choral traditions of ancient Greece.

From Bach to Beethoven:

The period between roughly 1600 and 1820 saw Western music transformed into a sophisticated art form. Baroque composers like Bach and Handel built towering works of counterpoint and religious drama, while the Classical era brought clarity and formal structure through the symphonies of Haydn and Mozart. Beethoven straddled both worlds, pushing music toward raw emotional power and paving the way for the Romantic era that followed.

The Modern Revolution:

The 20th century shattered music into dozens of directions at once. Jazz emerged from African American communities in New Orleans and reshaped popular music worldwide, while rock and roll electrified a new generation in the 1950s. By the end of the century, digital technology had democratised music-making entirely today, anyone with a laptop can produce and share music with a global audience, giving rise to an explosion of genres and sounds that continues to evolve.
